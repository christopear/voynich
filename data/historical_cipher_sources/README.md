# Historical codebook-size check

Accessed 9 October 2026. Bibliographic evidence only; no text is incorporated into
training, and no manuscript images or paper PDFs were downloaded.

Beáta Megyesi et al., *Keys with nomenclatures in the early modern Europe*,
Cryptologia 48(2), 97–139, published online 2022:
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01611194.2022.2113185),
[author-uploaded full text inspected](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/365079786_Keys_with_nomenclatures_in_the_early_modern_Europe).

This empirical study examines historical keys. Its summary reports that roughly
80% of fifteenth-century nomenclatures in the collection have fewer than 100
elements, roughly half fewer than 50. Section 9 explains that manual size
categories stop at over 100; the authors suggest finer 500/1,000 categories.
Thus it does **not** substantiate a 1,500–4,500-entry example around 1420.
Nor does its sample establish that such a codebook was impossible. A dated
archival key of the relevant size is still needed. Our recipe counts are
simulation sizes, not historical attestations. Fluent manual use is an additional
untested operational assumption.

Láng et al., *Cipher key instructions in early modern Europe: analysis and text
edition*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01611194.2024.2396800),
[institutional full text](https://uu.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1997149/FULLTEXT01.pdf),
is a lead for studying actual usage instructions; it is not a dated large-key
attestation for the present hypothesis.
