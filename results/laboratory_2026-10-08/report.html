5. Historical scope

describes Caesar's shifted-letter practice. Our exhaustive test instantiates that family on a modern 26-letter storage alphabet,
rather than claiming an exact reconstruction of Roman orthography.


Fifteenth-century letter/word substitution is documented in Penn's
LJS 225 catalogue, dated 1455–1458.
Marco Vito's HistoCrypt 2026 study
discusses homophones and nomenclators in the same treatise. These establish family-level feasibility around the mid-fifteenth century,
not attestation of our exact keys or a pre-1438 date for every feature.
